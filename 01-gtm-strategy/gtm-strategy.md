# GTM Strategy · Roamly Groups

## 1. Discover Framework

### Align on the Goal
- **Core business problem:** They are trying to enhance coordination of affairs between disaggregated groups of people (presumably friends or disparate family members) who are working to purchase an culinary and cultural experience in a local city.
- **What success looks like:** Increased usage of the platform, higher customer satisfaction, reduced friction (for booking), increase in overall purchases, increased stickiness (ongoing access of the booking similar to an e-vite, etc.), increased opportunities for ongoing engagement, upsell or new service creations.

### Do the Research
- **Signals of a real opportunity:** It seems that users are coming to the site to start a booking but drop-off rate is high. User data suggests that customers are already doing this off the site in confusing ways. Coordinating the cultural, culinary event is presumably harder work than finding the event you want to purchase.
- **Top 5 possible segments:** Definitely not individual or family. Need to focus on segments that would benefit from disparate member coordination: disparate family get togethers, strangers (that would gain an economic benefit from a larger booking - eg., uber's rideshare discount), individuals who are seeking additional social companionship on trips, celebratory events (like birthday parties -- it's always awkward to ask for $ after; the app would just do it for you).
- **How to size the opportunity:** MVP the feature set for feedback

### Build a Working Team
- **Cross-functional stakeholders:** The event organizer him / herself (the person running the event)
Former customers in the aforementioned customer segments
Financial / data team 
Data analysis (precise areas of drop-off and any feedback acquired throughout the process)

### Form Your Hypothesis
- **Most valuable segment to target first, and why:** Party goers - birthday, bachelor / ette, reunions, disparate vacationers (that know each other)
- **Core value proposition:** Fully coordinate end-to-end the selection of a culinary or cultural event in a particular city from selection, to RSVP, to communication, to payment.

## 4. The assumption I'm pushing back on

I would push back on the idea that I have to narrow my target market even more to just one primary segment: Local social organizers arranging prepaid culinary experiences for 6–12 friends who expect to split costs. The feature set as designed should not be limited to *local* only but anyone who can / is willing to travel to a new destination with a group that needs this feature set. It doesn't need to be geographically limited and in fact that might be difficult to do.

## 5. V1 GTM strategy one-pager

### Roamly Groups | GTM Strategy V1
**Product:** Coordinated group booking, split payments, and shared itineraries  
**Primary growth objective:** Expand  
**GTM motion:** Product-Led Growth (PLG)  
**Status:** Working draft | November 2026

#### 1. Business Opportunity
Roamly customers planning group culinary and cultural experiences face coordination friction across event selection, attendance, payments, and logistics. These activities often happen outside the platform, potentially contributing to booking abandonment and missed revenue.

**Opportunity:** Convert more group booking intent into completed purchases while creating additional engagement and customer acquisition opportunities through shared invitations.

#### 2. Target Market
**Primary audience:** People organizing shared culinary and cultural experiences for groups requiring coordinated attendance, decisions, and payments.

**Priority use cases:** Birthdays, bachelor/bachelorette celebrations, reunions, and group vacations.

**Geographic scope:** All supported Roamly destinations, whether participants are local residents or travelers.

**Segmentation principle:** Prioritize coordination complexity and independent payment needs, not geographic proximity.

#### 3. Value Proposition
**"Bring your group together. We'll handle the coordination."**

Roamly Groups simplifies planning and purchasing shared experiences through one place to invite participants, coordinate attendance, split payments, and access event details.

**Core differentiator:** Reduce the organizer's administrative and financial burden without requiring participants to manage multiple tools.

#### 4. Recommended GTM & Channel Approach

**Primary motion: PLG through existing booking intent**

- **On-site conversion:** Introduce "Book as a Group" on eligible experience pages and during checkout.
- **Invitation-led distribution:** Enable organizers to share booking links through text, WhatsApp, email, and existing group chats.
- **Lifecycle marketing:** Use email and in-app messaging to introduce Groups to customers with relevant past booking behavior.
- **Post-event acquisition:** Invite participating guests to organize their own future Roamly experiences.

**Secondary motion: Targeted demand generation**

Test occasion-specific messaging for birthdays, group vacations, and bachelor/bachelorette celebrations through organic content, SEO, and selective paid campaigns.

**Launch recommendation:** Make Groups accessible across supported destinations, but initially prioritize promotional placements and experimentation where group-booking intent is strongest.

#### 5. Success Metrics

**Primary metric: Incremental completed group bookings per 1,000 eligible booking-intent sessions.**

Compare customers offered Groups against a comparable control group using the existing checkout flow. This helps distinguish incremental bookings from transactions that would have happened anyway.

**Supporting metrics:**
- Booking conversion lift and incremental gross profit
- Invitation-to-payment completion rate
- Average group booking value
- Guest-to-organizer conversion within 90 days
- Customer satisfaction and payment-related support volume

**Initial success hypothesis:** Groups increases completed bookings and contribution profit without materially increasing payment failures, cancellations, or customer support costs.

#### 6. Validation & Launch Plan

**Phase 1 — Discover:** Analyze booking abandonment, interview group organizers, and validate payment/coordination friction as a meaningful conversion barrier.

**Phase 2 — Pilot:** Launch on eligible experiences, instrument the organizer and guest journeys, and test Group checkout against standard checkout.

**Phase 3 — Expand:** Scale winning placements and messages; test referral-driven acquisition and additional segments based on measured results.

#### 7. Key Assumptions & Risks
- Coordination friction is a meaningful cause of booking abandonment, not simply correlated with it.
- Invited guests will complete payments without excessive signup or commitment friction.
- Split payments won't introduce new booking failures that offset conversion gains.
- Group invitations can become a viable customer acquisition channel.

**Next decision:** Validate which group occasions and booking contexts have the highest recoverable revenue opportunity before committing a broader marketing budget.
